## What it does

For a uniform source on a rational convex polygon $K$ and two finite affine-max potentials $u$ and $v$, the package computes in exact rational arithmetic **how much the optimal transport maps differ**,
$$F(u,v)=\int_K\lvert\nabla u-\nabla v\rvert^2\,d\rho,$$
without confusing it with the optimal transport distance $W_2^2$ between the two target distributions, which it certifies separately by an exact primal–dual witness. When the coefficients of $v$ are only known to lie in a box, it returns

| Output | Meaning | Where proved |
|---|---|---|
| envelope $B$ | $F(u,\tilde v)\le B$ for **every** single realisation $\tilde v$ of the box; no fixed cell combinatorics or masses assumed | Theorem 2 (P3) |
| baseline $B_0$ | the elementary slope-diameter bound $\sum_i\rho(C_i)\max_jM_{ij}\ge B$ | equation (4) |
| tightness constant $\gamma(r)$ | $B-F(u,v_{\text{nominal}})\le\gamma(r)$, linear in the largest radius | Theorem 4 (P7) |
| interval $[\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB}]$ | $\mathrm{LB}\le\sup_{\tilde v}F(u,\tilde v)\le\mathrm{UB}$ at every budget, $\mathrm{LB}$ attained by an explicit realisation, width $\le\gamma_{\text{box}}(r_*)$ | Theorem 6 (P7) |

The envelope can be very loose: on the adverse family of Proposition 3, $B=4$ while the sharp maximum is $\varepsilon^2/4$ (P6). The subdivision interval repairs this (within 0.14 % at 256 sub-boxes). A two-parameter three-atom calibration (Theorem 7, Corollary 8) gives closed formulas for $F$, $W_2^2$ and their difference, with a corner maximum on admissible rectangles.

## Reproduce

Python 3.10 or later; standard library only. Tested with the interpreter recorded in [REPLAY_RECEIPT.md](REPLAY_RECEIPT.md); other versions are not implicitly certified.

```sh
./replay_archive.sh            # all of the following, in order
python3 test_transport.py
python3 -O test_transport.py
python3 transport_cli.py examples/tilt.json --check examples/tilt.result.json
python3 transport_cli.py examples/uncertainty.json --check examples/uncertainty.result.json
python3 transport_cli.py examples/refine.json --check examples/refine.result.json
python3 benchmark.py --check
python3 verify_manifest.py
```

The test suite (14 methods; 45 tilt cases, 24 generic two-algorithm cases, 32 sampled realisations, 169 grid points, 404 adverse-family grid cases, 108 tightness cases) takes a few seconds; `benchmark.py --check` recomputes the 65 benchmark cases (about one minute) and compares every exact field. Compilation is optional: `pdflatex -output-directory=paper paper/manuscript.tex` twice rebuilds the PDF from the included source and the two generated table files (`paper/benchmark_*.tex`, regenerated by `python3 benchmark.py --table`).

## Worked example: obtaining and reading a coefficient box

`examples/refine.json` is the adverse family with $\varepsilon=1/10$:

```json
{"domain": [[1,0],[2,0],[2,1],[1,1]],
 "reference": [[1,0,0]],
 "comparison": [[1,0,0],[0,0,"1/10"]],
 "radii": [[0,0,0],[1,0,0]],
 "refine": {"max_boxes": 256, "tolerance": "1/100000"}}
```

- `domain` is the source polygon (counterclockwise, convex, rational vertices); the source is uniform on it. Here it is the unit-area rectangle $[1,2]\times[0,1]$.
- `reference` lists the planes $[a_1,a_2,c]$ of $u=\max_i(a_i\cdot x+c_i)$; here $u(x)=x_1$, whose map sends everything to the point $(1,0)$.
- `comparison` lists the nominal planes of $v$ and `radii` the half-widths of the box around each coefficient: the first plane is fixed, the second has slope $b\in[0-1,0+1]$ in $x_1$ and intercept exactly $1/10$. A user obtains such a box from whatever bounds the coefficients: measurement tolerances on the target sites and dual weights of a semi-discrete problem, interval enclosures from a solver, or a design range. The induced target masses are an output (`second_masses`), not an input: this is not a solver for potentials with prescribed masses.
- `refine` asks for the subdivision interval with at most 256 sub-boxes or a width of $10^{-5}$.

Running `python3 transport_cli.py examples/refine.json` prints, among other fields,

```text
"map_distance_squared": "0"                 exact F at the nominal realisation (b=0: the second plane never wins)
"coefficient_box": {"map_distance_squared_upper": "4", "baseline_slope_diameter": "4", "arrangement_regions": 1}
"refined_box": {"lower": "41943/16777216", "upper": "88641311/35316039680",
                "attaining_realization": [["1","0","0"],["3891/4096","0","1/10"]], "converged": true, ...}
```

Read it as: every realisation has $F\le4$ (true but useless here), and in fact the worst case over the box lies in $[0.0024999,\,0.0025100]$, the lower end being reached at $b=3891/4096$ (the exact maximum is $1/400$ at $b=19/20$). Every number is an exact rational; `--check` reruns the computation and compares all fields with the same implementation (a reproducibility check, not an independent verification).

## Input and trust boundary

Integers and rational strings such as `"1/3"` are exact; binary floating-point values, malformed strings such as `"1/0"`, repeated reference slopes, invalid polygons and corrupt witnesses are refused with a diagnostic. Comparison planes may coincide and cells may disappear. Reaching the region cap raises an exception and returns no certificate; exhausting the refinement budget returns a wider but valid interval. The target-cost checker verifies a supplied coupling and dual vectors against the **recomputed** target masses by exact weak duality.

## Status and limitations

- Nothing here is formally verified; no unaffiliated party has rerun it; no specialist or journal has reviewed it.
- Version 0.1.0 (local, never public) received a same-model fresh-context internal review ([INTERNAL_REVIEW.md](INTERNAL_REVIEW.md), [REVIEW_DISPOSITION.md](REVIEW_DISPOSITION.md)) and an external review supplied by the publisher, which recommended major revisions; every item was actioned in 0.2.0 ([REVIEW_RESPONSE.md](REVIEW_RESPONSE.md)). The new results of 0.2.0 were sent to a cross-vendor model prompted to refute them ([STATUS.md](STATUS.md)).
- Power diagrams, common-source couplings, the possible-winner screening principle and the original three-atom example are credited existing material ([PRIOR_ART.md](PRIOR_ART.md)); historical priority is **not** claimed.
- The implementation is planar with a uniform source; the proofs of Theorems 2, 4 and 6 do not use the dimension, but no higher-dimensional or weighted backend exists.
- Uniform refinement costs a number of sub-boxes exponential in the box dimension $3n$; the benchmark shows it is decisive on one-parameter boxes and marginal at budget 32 on generic boxes.
